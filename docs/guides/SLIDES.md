# Slides

Slides opens, edits and saves `.pptx` presentations. It covers what most presentations need (layouts, text, shapes, lines, tables, pictures, a full-screen show); it is not a PowerPoint replacement (no animations).

## Working with slides
- The strip on the left shows the slides: click one to open it, drag one to reorder it. **Ctrl+M** (or Slide › New slide, or the + button) adds a slide; the button beside it picks one of eleven **layouts**, named as in Google Slides: Title slide, Title and body, Title and two columns, Title only, One column text, Main point, Section header, Section title and description, Caption, Big number, Blank. Each starts with empty placeholders ("Click to add a title"); an empty placeholder is never shown in the show or saved. **Slide › Apply layout** (or the *Layout* button) changes the layout of the slide you are on: your text moves into the matching boxes, and anything else (pictures, shapes, tables) stays where it is. **Slide › Change theme** (or the *Theme* button) gives the whole presentation a background, font and title colour in one step.
- On the slide, click an object to select it (**Shift+click** adds to the selection, dragging on the background draws a selection rectangle, **Ctrl+A** selects everything), drag to move it (pink guides snap it to the slide's edges and middle and to the other objects; hold **Alt** to move freely), drag a handle to resize it, drag the round handle above it to turn it (Shift snaps to 15°). Arrow keys nudge by 1, with Shift by 10. **Double-click a text box** to type in it; the toolbar's font, size, bold, italic, underline, colour, alignment, bullets, numbering and line spacing then work on the selected words. Select the box instead and they apply to all of its text.
- **Insert** a text box, a shape (rectangle, rounded rectangle, ellipse, triangle, diamond, hexagon, star, arrows), a **line** or an **arrow** (drag its two end handles), a **table** or a picture. A shape is a text box with a fill: type in it. Fill colour, outline and line colour, line weight, dashed lines and arrow heads are on the toolbar and the Format menu.
- **Tables:** double-click a cell to type (Tab goes to the next cell, Esc cancels), click a cell and use the **Table** menu to insert or delete rows and columns, turn the header row and banded rows on or off; bold, colours, size and alignment apply to the cell you are in (or the whole table if none is). Drag the table's handles to resize it with its rows and columns.
- **Arrange** (laid out as in Google Slides) has **Order** (bring to front, bring forward, send backward, send to back), **Align horizontally / vertically** (to the slide for one object, to each other for several), **Distribute** (three or more), **Center on page** and **Rotate**. The Format menu has Text, Align & indent, Line & paragraph spacing, Bullets & numbering and Borders & lines. **Slide › Background colour** and **Slide size** (16:9 or 4:3) are in the Slide menu.
- **Pictures:** Format › **Crop picture** cuts a percentage off each side (the picture itself is kept whole in the file's data, so you can reset it).
- **Charts:** Insert › **Chart** (or the chart button) makes a column, bar, line, area or pie chart from a small table of numbers you type or paste (first row: series names; first column: labels). Double-click a chart to change it. Charts are saved as real PowerPoint charts.
- **Merged table cells:** click a cell, then Table › Merge with the cell to the right / below; Unmerge cells splits it again.
- **Transitions:** Slide › **Transition** gives the slide how the show arrives at it (fade, push, wipe, zoom; "Apply this one to every slide"). They play in the show and are saved in the file.
- **Right-click** an object, a table cell, a slide in the strip or the empty slide for the usual menu (cut, copy, paste, duplicate, delete, order, align, rotate, table rows and columns, merge, apply layout, transition ...).
- Speaker notes go in the box under the slide (View › Speaker notes hides it).
- **Slideshow** (Ctrl+F5 from this slide, Ctrl+Shift+F5 from the beginning; F5 and Shift+F5 also work) shows the slides full screen: ←/→, Space or a click go on, Esc leaves.

## What is kept
Slides in order, text boxes and placeholders with their position, size, font, colour, bullets and alignment (including what they inherit from the slide layout, master and theme), numbering and line spacing, shapes (rectangle, rounded rectangle, ellipse, triangle, diamond, hexagon, five-point star, arrows) with fills and outlines, straight lines and arrows, tables (text, bold, italic, colour, fill, alignment, size, header and banded rows, merged cells), rotation, pictures with their cropping, column / bar / line / area / pie charts (their numbers), slide transitions, backgrounds and speaker notes. The file saved from here is a *new* presentation built from these, so it does **not** keep (and you are told when you open such a file): other charts, diagrams, animations, audio and video, comments, curved connectors and other shapes than the ones above (they become rectangles). A table's look comes from the file's own table style, which is approximated by a header colour and banding. The first **Save** over such a file asks you to agree; **Save as** keeps the original untouched; a copy of the original also goes to the app's `backups` folder.

Fonts follow the rule of Docs: the font box lists those installed on this computer, and a presentation can name one this computer lacks.
