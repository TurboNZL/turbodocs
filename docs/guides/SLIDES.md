# Slides

Slides opens, edits and saves `.pptx` presentations. It covers what most presentations need (layouts, text, shapes, lines, tables, pictures, a full-screen show); it is not a PowerPoint replacement (no animations, transitions or charts).

## Working with slides
- The strip on the left shows the slides: click one to open it, drag one to reorder it. **Slide › New slide** (or the layout button) offers nine **layouts**: title slide, title and content, section header, two columns, comparison, caption and content, title only, big statement and blank. Each starts with empty placeholders ("Click to add a title"); an empty placeholder is never shown in the show or saved. **Slide › Layout** (or the *Layout* button) changes the layout of the slide you are on: your text moves into the matching boxes, and anything else (pictures, shapes, tables) stays where it is. **Slide › Design** gives the whole presentation a background, font and title colour in one step.
- On the slide, click an object to select it (**Shift+click** adds to the selection, dragging on the background draws a selection rectangle, **Ctrl+A** selects everything), drag to move it (pink guides snap it to the slide's edges and middle and to the other objects; hold **Alt** to move freely), drag a handle to resize it, drag the round handle above it to turn it (Shift snaps to 15°). Arrow keys nudge by 1, with Shift by 10. **Double-click a text box** to type in it; the toolbar's font, size, bold, italic, underline, colour, alignment, bullets, numbering and line spacing then work on the selected words. Select the box instead and they apply to all of its text.
- **Insert** a text box, a shape (rectangle, rounded rectangle, ellipse, triangle, diamond, hexagon, star, arrows), a **line** or an **arrow** (drag its two end handles), a **table** or a picture. A shape is a text box with a fill: type in it. Fill colour, outline and line colour, line weight, dashed lines and arrow heads are on the toolbar and the Format menu.
- **Tables:** double-click a cell to type (Tab goes to the next cell, Esc cancels), click a cell and use the **Table** menu to insert or delete rows and columns, turn the header row and banded rows on or off; bold, colours, size and alignment apply to the cell you are in (or the whole table if none is). Drag the table's handles to resize it with its rows and columns.
- **Arrange** has bring to front and send to back, **align** (left, centre, right, top, middle, bottom: to the slide for one object, to each other for several), **distribute** (three or more) and rotate 90°. Duplicate (Ctrl+D) and delete are on the toolbar. **Slide › Background colour** and **Slide size** (16:9 or 4:3) are in the Slide menu.
- Speaker notes go in the box under the slide (View › Speaker notes hides it).
- **Present** (F5, or Shift+F5 from the current slide) shows the slides full screen: ←/→, Space or a click go on, Esc leaves.

## What is kept
Slides in order, text boxes and placeholders with their position, size, font, colour, bullets and alignment (including what they inherit from the slide layout, master and theme), numbering and line spacing, shapes (rectangle, rounded rectangle, ellipse, triangle, diamond, hexagon, five-point star, arrows) with fills and outlines, straight lines and arrows, tables (text, bold, italic, colour, fill, alignment, size, header and banded rows), rotation, pictures, backgrounds and speaker notes. The file saved from here is a *new* presentation built from these, so it does **not** keep (and you are told when you open such a file): charts and diagrams, animations and transitions, audio and video, picture cropping, comments, merged table cells, curved connectors and other shapes than the ones above (they become rectangles). A table's look comes from the file's own table style, which is approximated by a header colour and banding. The first **Save** over such a file asks you to agree; **Save as** keeps the original untouched; a copy of the original also goes to the app's `backups` folder.

Fonts follow the rule of Docs: the font box lists those installed on this computer, and a presentation can name one this computer lacks.
