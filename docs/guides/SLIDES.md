# Slides

Slides opens, edits and saves `.pptx` presentations. It is a plain, usable editor, not a PowerPoint replacement.

## Working with slides
- The strip on the left shows the slides: click one to open it, drag one to reorder it. **Slide › New slide** offers a title slide, a title with bullets, or a blank one; Duplicate, Delete and Move are in the same menu.
- On the slide, click an object to select it, drag to move it, drag a handle to resize it (arrow keys nudge by 1, with Shift by 10). **Double-click a text box** to type in it; the toolbar's font, size, bold, italic, underline, colour, alignment and bullets then work on the selected words. Select the box instead and they apply to all of its text.
- **Insert** a text box, a rectangle or an ellipse (a shape is a text box with a fill: type in it), or a picture. Fill colour, bring to front, send to back, duplicate (Ctrl+D) and delete are on the toolbar. **Slide › Background colour** and **Slide size** (16:9 or 4:3) are in the Slide menu.
- Speaker notes go in the box under the slide (View › Speaker notes hides it).
- **Present** (F5, or Shift+F5 from the current slide) shows the slides full screen: ←/→, Space or a click go on, Esc leaves.

## What is kept
Slides in order, text boxes and placeholders with their position, size, font, colour, bullets and alignment (including what they inherit from the slide layout, master and theme), rectangles and ellipses with fills and outlines, pictures, backgrounds and speaker notes. The file saved from here is a *new* presentation built from these, so it does **not** keep (and you are told when you open such a file): tables, charts and diagrams, animations and transitions, audio and video, rotated shapes, lines and connectors, picture cropping, comments, and other shapes than rectangles and ellipses (they become rectangles). The first **Save** over such a file asks you to agree; **Save as** keeps the original untouched; a copy of the original also goes to the app's `backups` folder.

Fonts follow the rule of Docs: the font box lists those installed on this computer, and a presentation can name one this computer lacks.
